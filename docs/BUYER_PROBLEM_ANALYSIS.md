# Buyer problem analysis

**Status: hypotheses to validate, not customer discovery. Research date: 18 November 2026.** No interviews, customer commitments, product usage, willingness-to-pay evidence, or quantified buyer losses are claimed. The live [Gulf Air Group tender](DEMAND_SIGNALS.md) is evidence of demand for treasury software broadly, not evidence of this product's precise workflow.

## Candidate buyer categories

| Category | Plausible operational problem and frequency | Current system / workaround | Problem owner and budget | Data and integrations | Purchase case for a separate layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-border payment operators / PSPs | Several payout obligations compete for prefunded balances or constrained route windows; potentially daily, but frequency and loss are **Unknown**. | Payment hub, internal routing rules, provider auto-routing, manual treasury escalation or spreadsheets (hypothesis). | Payments operations / treasury own workflow; COO, CFO or payments product may sponsor. Budget ownership unverified. | Decision-time balances/encumbrances, obligations, provider quotes/caps/cutoffs, outcomes; payment engine, ledger, TMS and provider APIs. | Only if external-provider allocation produces a measurable improvement unavailable in their hub and does not compromise service or control. |
| Payment facilitators / marketplaces | Timing of seller/merchant payouts and funding may conflict; split-fund and payout rules may be present. | Platform ledger and payout providers; manual holds or batch timing (hypothesis). | Payments operations, finance, risk; budget unverified. | Seller obligations, available safeguarded/operating balances, eligibility, payout promise and exceptions; platform ledger and PSPs. | A separate planner is unlikely unless it spans several providers or legal entities and preserves contractual payout rules. |
| Payment orchestration platforms | Customers may ask for portfolio-level planning across connectors, but current systems may already support routing rules. | Orchestration engine, configured routing and provider-specific failover. | Product/engineering or platform operations; budget unverified. | Connector-level quotes, route health/caps, customer policy, payment status; orchestration API and treasury data. | A reusable module could be embedded if multiple customers independently need it; no evidence of that demand yet. |
| Stablecoin payment providers | Liquidity across on/off-ramp, fiat and digital-asset legs could be fragmented; operational frequency and consequences **Unknown**. | Treasury/settlement systems, liquidity desks and route/on-ramp orchestration. | Treasury, settlement ops and risk; budget unverified. | Asset/currency balances, conversion quotes, prefunding, chain/rail finality and legal constraints; custody, exchange, banking and reconciliation systems. | Only with explicit asset, jurisdiction and control boundaries; current EUR-only model is far from sufficient. |
| Enterprise treasury / shared services | Cash visibility, payment centralization, cash pooling and forecasting across banks/entities are recognized software needs; competing route capacity is not demonstrated as a distinct pain. | TMS, ERP/AP, bank portals, payment factory, cash forecasts, in-house bank/netting, spreadsheets. | Group treasurer / treasury operations; CFO or finance transformation typically sponsors (category inference). | Entity/account balances, commitments, payment files, cutoffs, bank fees/FX and settlement evidence; ERP, TMS, banks and reconciliation. | Likely incumbent module unless existing TMS demonstrably cannot plan across bank/provider constraints. |
| Financial infrastructure / banking providers | Could expose capacity, cash and route services to downstream participants; demand for a separate planner unknown. | Internal treasury, scheme/rail management and customer APIs. | Product, network operations or treasury; budget unknown. | Capacity/availability, liquidity positions, service policies, customer obligations; core banking, payment engine and ledger. | A partnership or embedded capability may fit better than a direct standalone sale. |

## Qualification questions for real discovery

Ask an operational owner to walk through the last actual constrained funding/window decision: what was competing, who decided, what system showed balances and route eligibility, whether a limit bound, what happened to any deferred/failed obligation, how often it recurs, and how the result was reconciled. Request artifacts (redacted decision log, policy, sample data dictionary) rather than broad opinions. Ask who signs for a read-only evaluation and what procurement/security review is required. Do not lead with “optimization” or savings.

## Highest-fit initial segment (hypothesis)

**Inference:** a multi-provider cross-border PSP/payment operator with a centralized treasury function is the narrowest plausible user: it may have daily obligations, provider choice and shared prefunding, and could evaluate a planner without replacing its payment stack. This remains a target hypothesis. Public documents establish that major platforms offer internal routing and TMS products; they do not establish an underserved buyer or a reachable budget owner.

## Evidence gaps

Unknowns include frequency of binding shared constraints, current use of spreadsheets/manual overrides, the relevant economic cost, whether the incumbent can already solve it, decision rights across providers, availability of historical data, appetite for an external planning layer, security/procurement burden, and willingness to pay. Each must be tested with specific operators before positioning a commercial product.
