# Differentiation assessment

**Research date: 18 November 2026.** This assessment tests the project thesis against public vendor material summarized in [Competitor Deep Dive](COMPETITOR_DEEP_DIVE.md). Status labels: **Verified** direct public evidence; **Inference** interpretation of that evidence; **Hypothesis** unvalidated proposition; **Unknown** unresolved.

## What is and is not different

**Verified in this demonstrator:** it jointly allocates synthetic obligations against one shared EUR-equivalent liquidity amount and scenario-level route caps, while applying priority and deadline eligibility. The capacity-tradeoff fixture demonstrates a feasible allocation where independent proposals overbook one route.

**Not established:** production portfolio optimization, live balance/quote accuracy, cross-currency/entity funding, real route-capacity feeds, improved on-time outcomes, reduced cost, reduced prefunding, or customer demand. The planner searches bounded full-payment combinations, then greedily adds partial allocations; it is not a globally optimal mixed full/partial multi-leg allocator. It does not execute or reconcile.

## Commoditized and potentially differentiated

- **Already present in market:** choosing rails/routes; configured routing rules; provider-internal cost/speed/coverage routing; multicurrency balance visibility; cutoffs/windows; payment status, audit and reconciliation; cash forecasts and scenario analysis. Named examples and links are in the matrix.
- **Potentially distinctive but unverified:** joint allocation of competing obligations across shared liquidity and heterogeneous external-provider capacity, with a transparent pre-execution explanation. Public sources reviewed did not verify this exact combination, but that is a documentation gap, not proof of absence.
- **Potentially irrelevant:** a lower modeled fee without equal funded value, service, deadline, risk and data conditions; a feasible plan when route limits are hypothetical; a deterministic explanation without an operator changing a decision.

## Competitive threat and verdict implication

The strongest threat is not a clone of this exact demo; it is a buyer’s existing TMS/payment hub combined with provider-specific auto-routing, rules, balance controls and operational workarounds. Kyriba and TIS are especially adjacent; Modern Treasury is relevant to API-centered payment operations. Nium, Airwallex and Thunes make route selection itself a weak novelty claim.

**Inference:** if the capability survives validation, it is more credible first as a module/decision service attached to an existing TMS, payment hub, or PSP workflow than as a standalone execution platform. The wedge would need to be vendor-neutral portfolio replay/shadow planning where the buyer can prove that incumbent tools cannot coordinate decisions across providers. Current evidence does not prove that gap exists at a buyer.
